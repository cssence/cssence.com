After what will hopefully have been my final hospital visit for the forseeable future, let me acknowledge that a hospital operates like a well-oiled machine. That said, I’m fairly certain that AI will be or already has been added to hospital processes in some shape or form. Makes you wonder how this will turn out, given that mistakes may result in life-or-death situations.

This is the final part in a series that claims to be about edge cases in web design, and it takes an unexpected turn:
Web design itself will become an edge case.

Before we dive in, I need to express where I stand on the whole AI debate, because until now I haven’t done that. In a nutshell, I find the technology impressive, but in it’s current state it comes with too many unaddressed problems. The only course of action is to avoid it. To this day I have not used it.

My number one reason to not use AI is the environmental impact, but there are many others. I see more and more people writing down their reservations. The most noteworthy one is “Why I Resist Generative ‘AI’” from Esther Weidauer, and you should read it asap, if you haven’t already.

And yet.

I’m a big fan of YouTuber Christopher Barnatt, who runs the Explaining Computers channel. In his recent video “Radical Computing Evolution”, he changed his position on (agentic) AI, and while it makes my stomach churn, I have to agree with him and change my own position too.

Even though my position on AI is changing, I will continue not using it, but there’s something fundamental going on that needs to be discussed.

The GUI is dying

Christopher predicts that Graphical User Interfaces (GUIs) will be replaced by Agent Command Interfaces (ACIs). Eventually operating systems will adapt to the new reality. This kind of evolution is not unheard of, the Graphical User Interfaces did replace Command Line Interfaces (CLIs) back in the day. Granted, CLIs never went away entirely. Many nerds, myself included, keep using them. But laypeople have no idea what a command line is.

We may now see the next transition, and eventually GUIs will share the fate of CLIs.

Why? Because the majority of people won’t mind. They want ease of access, and GUIs are frankly surprisingly often not up to the task, especially when compared to conversational interfaces. Agentic AI is taking the burden away from the user? What’s not to like?

The WWW is dying

The World Wide Web (WWW) is the Graphical User Interface of the Internet, a web browser provides the window to this world to the user. And it too may no longer be needed, when users no longer look through the window.

Christopher made another thought-provoking video, “AI & the Future of the Web”. In there he says:

For over 25 years, people have used search engines and primarily Google to search for and then visit web pages. And this has allowed a lot of websites to offer free access because they’ve been able to earn revenue from advertising. But since 2024, AI overviews have begun to dramatically disrupt the advertising-funded search and visit web business model. Conduct a web search today and a Google AI often provides a summary of what you want to know at the top of the page. And this means that an increasing number of users no longer click on a link and visit the website from which information has been sourced.

He then quotes a study, stating that in the year after Google launched AI overviews, organic traffic to sites had dropped significantly. Christopher continues:

It’s therefore not surprising that some fear that we are headed towards Google zero or the moment when Google search simply stops sending traffic outside of its search engine to third-party websites. And indeed, when I asked a Google AI about the impact of AI summaries, it reported how they are fundamentally dismantling the open web’s traditional business model by shifting search behavior from link clicking to answer consuming.

I’ve dealt with GUIs most of my life, and have therefore grown quite fond of them, and I enjoy creating them. But this only applies to my nerdy side. As a regular citizen and consumer I happen to have a different mindset. As I wrote in part three of this series, the WWW is broken, sometimes accidentally, sometimes on purpose. Therefore, using the WWW to complete a task can be frustrating. I have a family with three children and a job. In my nonexistent spare time I do parts of the jobs of 25 other people that I now can/must do because everything is made available online: Booking appointments, managing your finances, planning your vacation, the list seems endless.

  • Remember the time when you’ve been told that an unexpected error occured after having filled in all your personal data in an attempt to get an appointment for a haircut?
  • Remember the time when a discount price was nowhere to be found again because a session had expired?
  • Remember the time when a hotel room was no longer available after leaving a browser tab unattended a little too long?
  • Remember the time when you needed to find the opening hours of a restaurant, essential information on a restaurant website one might think, only to later realize that the website was designed in a way that buried the information in a place hard to find.

Why deal with all this yourself, when agentic AI can be put to the task? At this point, designing a website is no longer part of the equation. Agentic AI does not need a GUI, it only needs access to the information under the hood. Or, as Christopher put it:

I strongly suspect that in the future, most people will instruct an AI agent to answer a question, perform a task, or present some content rather than launching a web browser and directly accessing the internet themselves. And given that the whole reason for the World Wide Web to exist is to present a human interface to internet content, this will remove the web’s reason to exist. Or to put it another way, when most people access the internet indirectly using an AI agent, there will be no reason for a World Wide Web graphical interface and websites formatted for human use.

Will we see the AI bubble burst? Probably. Even if, it will only defer the transition.

Not too long ago I would have agreed with Michelle Barker: Maybe we’ll wake up to find little websites everywhere. Maybe. These days, I suspect that designing and creating websites will cease to be something you can do to make a living. Instead it will become some kind of retro hobby, a vintage craft.
